# House style and preproduction

Use these as recurring defaults for this user's video projects. Adapt them to each brief and reference; they are not a requirement to repeat the same palette, layout, or effects.

## Hook, script, and storyboard

- Every video plan includes an opening hook in the script. Give the hook a clear purpose: raise a relevant question, show a compelling result, or establish a useful tension or promise.
- Carry the hook into the storyboard as the opening visual and motion beat. State what appears, how it moves, and how that beat leads into the rest of the story. Add an audio cue only when it supports the moment.
- Make the hook and the later content agree. Avoid clickbait and claims the video does not support.
- Present the script, beat-by-beat storyboard, frame/timestamp motion breakdown, and sound plan before coding. Wait for the user's approval before implementation.
- No separate writing skill is needed for a Remotion video brief: handle script, storyboard, and VO direction in this preproduction workflow. Reconsider a separate skill only if the user establishes a broader writing workflow beyond video production.

## Vertical-video safe areas

- Ask or infer whether the target is TikTok, Instagram Reels, or another placement. Safe areas vary by platform, ad/organic placement, interface, caption length, and interactive overlays; do not present one rectangle as universally valid.
- For TikTok ads, use the current official safe-zone template for the exact format and check the platform preview. For Instagram Reels ads, Meta's placement guidance has used approximate margins of 14% from the top, 35% from the bottom, and 6% from each side. Treat those as an ad-placement reference, not a guarantee for every organic post or interface state.
- Keep essential copy, faces, logos, and calls to action clear of likely interface overlays. If the exact target placement is unknown, use a conservative central safe area and flag that it needs a placement preview.
- Guides or overlays used to check safe areas are production aids and must not appear in the finished video.

## Recurring motion and audio preferences

- Preserve a polished, editorial motion character: clear typographic hierarchy, purposeful stagger, responsive but restrained spring movement for UI-like elements, controlled emphasis, and soft settling for floating elements. Let the project brief and reference set the actual design; do not force a house palette or duplicate a reference composition.
- Default to original, restrained UI sound effects made for the project when sound design is wanted. Do not assume voice-over or background music is required.
- The user supplies background music when desired; if none is supplied, continue without it. Shape music around the voice-over when both are present, keeping speech clear.
- When voice-over is requested, provide the narration script and delivery direction (tone, pace, emphasis, and pauses) for the user to record. Do not imply that a recording exists unless one is provided.
- Tie each sound effect to the visible onset or impact in the frame plan, normally within a frame or two, then verify synchronization in the Remotion preview.
- Include an audio cue/timing plan in the pre-code review, even when the plan is to use no audio.

## VO-first timing and scene breathing room

- Inspect supplied voice-over before timing the edit: note each file's duration, actual spoken-word onsets, speech endings, breaths, and meaningful pauses. Use waveform or careful playback; do not infer word timing from clip length alone.
- Keep the recording at its natural speed. If the voice-over exceeds the requested runtime, explain the difference and ask whether to preserve the take or revise the script before implementation.
- Build the runtime from the voice-over, intentional inter-sentence gaps, scene transitions, and ending hold. Add pauses only where the delivery benefits; around 0.3–0.5 seconds can be a starting point, not a fixed rule.
- Plan every scene as entrance, action, settle, readable hold, and exit. Let the spoken phrase and the scene's meaningful movement finish before fading out. Use pauses to hold or transition, and do not leave logos or other scene elements visible without narrative reason.
- When the user requests word-by-word Apple-style captions, reveal each word near its audible onset and bring in a related visual when its subject is spoken. Keep a word pop within one or two frames of the reveal; reduce its level and density so frequent pops stay subordinate to the voice.
- In preview, inspect the frames just before and after every sentence, scene change, and VO pause. Confirm captions, related visuals, and SFX have completed or exited cleanly, including during silence.
